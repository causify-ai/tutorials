## User Research and Market Understanding

* More on a solving a problem
- You are in trouble if you can't articulate an answer to the 2 questions:
  - Why will users use this product?
  - What problem does it solve? For whom?
- Think in terms of a target customer
  - E.g., a customer that day trades NFTs, "Nathan lives in Virginia, has
    disposable income, every day he checks Reddit"
  - Make your product delightful for that user

- How bad is the problem?
  - Is this a "nice-to-have" or a "must-have"?
  - Does it save costs or increase revenue?

* Common ideation mistakes
- Building for customers who don't exist yet
  - Almost always a mistake
  - Without real customers you don't get feedback (which kills startups),
    without feedback
- Solving yesterday's problems, rather than tomorrow's problems
  - By the time you solve the problem it's going to be way to late
  - E.g., "build a platform for better ICOs", but ICOs were a thing of 2020 and
    nobody is doing ICOs in 2023
- Building for crypto influencers
  - People of Twitter complain about "why there isn't XYZ?"
  - He is famous
  - I'm going to build XYZ

* Are you building the right thing?
- Build a proof of concept
- Show it off online or at a hackathon
- Many crypto projects were built without funding, hacked together
  - E.g., Uniswap, 1Inch
