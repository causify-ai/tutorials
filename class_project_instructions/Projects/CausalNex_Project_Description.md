### Project 1: Causal Analysis of Air Quality and Public Health Outcomes
- **Difficulty:** 1
- **Tech Description:** CausalNex will be used to build a causal model that connects air quality metrics with public health outcomes using observational data.
- **Project Idea:** This project aims to explore the causal relationship between air quality indicators (e.g., PM2.5, NO2) and respiratory health outcomes (e.g., asthma hospitalizations). Using publicly available datasets from the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the student will construct a causal graph using CausalNex to identify key causal pathways and potential interventions. The analysis will help in understanding how improvements in air quality can lead to better public health outcomes.
- **Python libs:** CausalNex, Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, StatsModels
- **Is it Free?** Yes, both the tool and the datasets used are freely available.
- **Relevant tool (CausalNex) related Resource Links:** 
  - [CausalNex Documentation](https://causalnex.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)
  - [EPA Air Quality Data](https://www.epa.gov/airdata)

### Project 2: Causal Inference in Marketing Campaign Effectiveness
- **Difficulty:** 2
- **Tech Description:** CausalNex will help in constructing a causal model to evaluate the effectiveness of different marketing channels on sales.
- **Project Idea:** This project focuses on understanding how various marketing channels (e.g., email, social media, and online ads) causally influence product sales. By utilizing data from a public e-commerce dataset (such as the UCI Machine Learning Repository's Online Retail dataset), the student will use CausalNex to build a causal graph that explores the relationships and dependencies among marketing activities and sales performance. The project will provide insights into which channels yield the highest return on investment and inform future marketing strategies.
- **Python libs:** CausalNex, Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn
- **Is it Free?** Yes, the tool and the dataset are freely accessible.
- **Relevant tool (CausalNex) related Resource Links:** 
  - [CausalNex GitHub Repository](https://github.com/capitalone/CausalNex)
  - [UCI Online Retail Dataset](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/ml/datasets/Online+Retail)

### Project 3: Understanding the Impact of Socioeconomic Factors on Crime Rates
- **Difficulty:** 3
- **Tech Description:** CausalNex will be utilized to build a complex causal model to analyze the interplay between socioeconomic factors and crime rates.
- **Project Idea:** This advanced project aims to assess how various socioeconomic factors (e.g., unemployment rates, education levels, income inequality) causally affect crime rates in urban areas. The student will gather data from the FBI's Uniform Crime Reporting (UCR) Program and the U.S. Census Bureau. Using CausalNex, the student will develop a sophisticated causal graph to explore the interactions and feedback loops among these factors. The goal is to derive actionable insights for policymakers to design effective crime prevention strategies based on causal reasoning.
- **Python libs:** CausalNex, Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, NetworkX
- **Is it Free?** Yes, both the tool and datasets are freely available to the public.
- **Relevant tool (CausalNex) related Resource Links:** 
  - [CausalNex Tutorials](https://causalnex.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials.html)
  - [FBI UCR Program](https://www.fbi.gov/services/cjis/ucr) 
  - [U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/)
