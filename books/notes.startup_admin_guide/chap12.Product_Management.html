    Product Development and Market Fit#

    • Product
    • A tool or utility that solves a need
    • Sometimes the problem solves a problem that it is different than what it is actually solving
    • E.g., Facebook "make the world more connected" vs "need of humans to show off" and "do something when you are bored"
    • The best product usually doesn't win
    • What's difficult is how to acquire users

    • Markets are pretty unfair because there are companies with unfair advantages and are willing to use it

    • Needs

    • You need to understand which of the 7 deadly sins your product is catering to

    • There are few human drivers

    • Lust (vs chastity)
    • Gluttony (vs temperance)
    • Greed (vs generosity)
    • Sloth (vs diligence)
    • Wrath (vs patience)
    • Envy (vs gratitude)
    • Pride (vs humility)

    • The human drivers do not change

    • Humans spend a lot of money to signal to other humans that they are "fit" (your DNA is great and your offspring will be fantastic)

    • Luxury goods
    • In the real world, it's through clothes, the restaurants you go to (that's one of the values of Instagram)
    • NFTs v1 can solve the same need (e.g., the Bored Ape)

    • NFTs v2: NFTs are a tech solution to the fact that your most important possessions are all digital (e.g., photos, an entry in a DB to represent that you went to Berkeley)

    • Product-market fit

    • = you have something that is so excellent that the market is going to pull it out of you
    • = a product that the market demands so much that the builders of the product will succeed despite their best efforts to fail
    • E.g., Twitter (they can barely keep the website up and they are worth $40B)

    • Success is easy if you have product-market fit

    • "Why Now?" is the most important question

    • Markets are pretty efficients and they prevent a certain innovation from succeeding / existing for a reason 1) New technology
    • E.g., battery technology enabled electric cars 2) New user acquisition channel
    • E.g., search / SEO
    • Tokenomics 3) New business model
    • Advertising supports free content online 4) Customer behavior has shifted
    • People understand the consequences of permanent identity online -> ephemerality
    • Put your information online to make it searchable by others -> social networking 5) New regulation

    • Obamacare

    • How to do product validation?

    • Deeply understand your market
    • Deeply understand your user / human need
    • Learn (aka fail) as quickly as possible
    • The company that moves is the one that moves faster

    • That's why startups win, it's because they can move and fail faster

    • People think that success is a straight line

    • In reality success is full of wrong turns

    • It's like failing all the times and feels terrible

    • Founder-product and founder-market fit

    • Because running a startup feels terrible, it's important that the founder has certain characteristics
    • E.g., the best products are created by people who are obsessed with the problem, the solution, the market and NOT with the outcome

    • It needs to be the mission of your life

    • Of the millions of people who could build this, why is it you that will build it?

    • Of the millions of products you could build, why is this the only product that your life should be dedicated to?
    • E.g., Elon Musk is obsessed with space
