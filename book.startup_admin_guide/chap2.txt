# I-Corps Next Day 2 Notes


## Startup Mistakes — Top 10
- Not having the right co-founders (36% of failures)
- Hiring poorly
- Lack of focus
- Building something nobody wants
- Failure to execute sales & marketing
- Chasing investors, not customers
- Not making sure you have enough money
- Spending too much money
- Failing to ask for help
- Ignoring social media

## Evidence-Based Discovery
- Evidence comes from discovery
  - Primarily through **interviews**
  - **Data is not evidence** without structure

## Clinical Example: Cardiogenic Shock

### Determine Etiology:
- ECG, echocardiography
- Arterial line BP monitoring
- Right heart catheterization
- Lactate & organ function markers

### Pharmacological Management:
- **Pre/Early Shock**
  - SBP < 100mmHg
  - HR 70–100
  - Normal lactate
  - Cool extremities
- **Shock**
  - SBP < 90mmHg
  - HR > 100
  - Lactate > 2
  - Altered mental status
- **Severe Shock**
  - SBP < 90mmHg
  - HR > 120
  - Lactate > 4
  - Obtunded

### Underlying Causes:
- Acute MI — 32% (STEMI 59%, NSTEMI 41%)
- Cardiomyopathy — 30% (hypertrophic, dilated, autoimmune, etc.)
- Ischemic heart disease — 19%
- Other — 18%

## Industry Ecosystems
- Identifies key players in the industry
- Helps target interviews
- Shows industry-level jobs and needs
- Maps different business model options

### Examples:
- **Value Chain — Magnet Wire for EVs**
- **Methanol Production Ecosystem**

## Customer Stakeholder Ecosystem
- Different customer segments = different business models
- Stakeholder roles:
  - **End Users:** interact directly with product
  - **Decision Makers:** make financial/use decisions
  - **Other Influencers:** key influence on adoption
  - **Beneficiaries:** benefit but have little influence
  - **KOLs/Experts:** influential professionals

## Workflows
- How the job is done today
- Identify key job steps & bottlenecks
- Useful for “before/after” state to show improvement

### Example:
- Wastewater Treatment Workflow:
  - Screening
  - Grit removal
  - Sludge separation
  - Phosphorous removal
  - Final treatment/discharge

## Value Propositions
- An **offer of value** to a customer
- Not just features/specs — must address **customer needs**
- Test value by understanding:
  - The job your customer hires your solution to do
  - Their **decision criteria** for adoption
  - How much better your solution needs to be to drive switching behavior

## Testing Needs & Solutions
- Start by identifying **unmet needs**
- Use decision criteria to test and validate value
- Understand what improvement is needed for adoption

# I-Corps Next Day 3 Notes

## What Makes a Good Beachhead Market?
- Customer has a compelling reason/need to buy
- Target customer is accessible to your sales force/channel
- Target customer is well-funded and able to pay
- You can deliver a full solution or find the right partners
- Consider if there are entrenched competitors
- Look for related/adjacent segments for future expansion
- Ensure the market aligns with the passion, interests, and goals of the founders

## Ecosystems: Industry & Value Chain
- Identify key industry players and interview targets
- Understand industry-level “jobs” and process steps
- Map evidence from customer discovery interviews
- Reveal different business model options based on direct customer choice
- Example players: EPCs, Catalyst Suppliers, Feedstock Suppliers, Off-takers, Regulators, Industry Associations

## Customer Stakeholder Map
- Shows typical company or consumer types
- Identifies key stakeholders and typical job titles (B2B)
- Assesses balance of influence — who has the most say
- Shows “customer of the customer” for component solutions

## Workflows
- Understand how the job is done today
- Identify key steps in larger processes
- Show “competing jobs”
- Visualize process owners and bottlenecks
- Useful for showing before/after states for improvements

## Channels
- How will you sell to, deliver to, and support customers?
- Identify direct vs. indirect sales channels
  - Direct: sell directly to end-use customer
  - Indirect: use channel partners, distributors, resellers
- Each intermediary in a channel expects to get paid
- Channel partners are motivated by money and ease
- Product complexity drives channel complexity

### Three High-Level Channel Functions
- Sales
- Distribution
- Support

## Customer Relationships
- Define how you will Get, Keep, and Grow customers
- Understand Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
  - Includes marketing and sales costs
  - Should align with solution and channel complexity
- High touch vs. low touch sales models have different CAC ranges

### Customer Acquisition Cost vs. Sales Complexity (Examples)
- Freemium/No Touch: $0–$10 CAC
- Self-Service/Inside Sales: $50–$2,000 CAC
- High Touch/Field Sales with SEs: $25,000–$200,000 CAC

## Revenue Streams & Pricing
- Identify how you will make money (asset sale, usage fees, subscription, licensing, rental, advertising, upsell)
- Align pricing models with customer expectations
  - Examples: cost plus, competitive pricing, volume pricing, value-based pricing
- Understand customer buying habits and budgeting cycles
- Calculate Lifetime Value (LTV) to ensure it is several multiples of CAC to cover costs and profit

## Key Discovery Questions to Ask Customers
- How do you typically purchase products? (asset, lease, subscription)
- How do purchases get authorized? At what price point does it get complicated?
- What justifications are needed for budget approval?
- What is the cost of not having a better solution?
- What does a typical budgeting cycle look like for a purchase like this?

## Payment Flows in the Ecosystem
- Map out how money moves between stakeholders
- Understand decision-making roles: champions, committees, OEMs, regulators
- Plan service contracts, one-time sales, and annual agreements

## Breakouts & Next Steps
- Map your channels: direct or indirect? Physical or digital? Who are intermediaries?
- Define your initial hypotheses for customer acquisition and revenue generation
- Use discovery interviews to validate your assumptions on market, channels, relationships, and pricing
