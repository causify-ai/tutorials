# I-Corps Next Day 1 Notes

## Why?

- Startups often fail for preventable reasons
- The goal is to help you avoid the biggest mistakes:
  - Building something nobody wants
  - Hiring poorly
  - Losing focus
- The goal is to help you shift from **strikeouts** to **base hits**, and maybe
  even **home runs**

## Build, Show, and Hope?

- "Build it and they will come" is not a strategy
- Showing your tech isn't enough
- Hope is not a business model
- Instead, you need to start with **evidence**, not assumptions

## Your goal

- Improve your odds of success
- Learn to build what customers *actually* need
- Discover how to test your assumptions, not just your product

- Evidence comes from structured discovery
- Discovery starts with conversations
  - Those conversations start with the **right questions**

## Desirable / Feasible / Viable

- A successful business needs all three:
  - **Desirable**: Customers want it
  - **Feasible**: You can build it
  - **Viable**: It can sustain itself financially

## Different Customer Segments = Different Business Models

- Not all customers are the same
- Each segment may:
  - Value different features
  - Require different pricing
  - Have different sales cycles
- Discovery helps you find the best **beachhead** to land

## Don't Fool Yourself

- It's difficult to be objective when it's something
  - about your business
  - you are an expert, you have a strong priors
  - you have dedicated and dedicate most of your day

## Customers Hire Solutions to Get Jobs Done

- "People don't want a quarter inch drill. They want a quarter inch hole.",
  Theodore Levitt
- Understand the **job** your customer is trying to accomplish
- Focus on outcomes, not tools

- You can't predict whether your customers will want a solution, unless you
  understand their needs first

*
- What is the job that somebody would hire your solution for:
  - "remove phosphorus from wastewater"
- Who would perform this job with your solution?
  - Waste water manager?
- Where might we find these people
  - B2C
    - "We sell solutions to individuals for personal use"
    - Our customers = consumer group
  - B2B Commercial
    - "We sell solutions to companies for use in their operations"
    - Our customers = decision makers, direct end-users
  - B2B Industrial
    - "We sell components / ingredients to other companies to embed in their
      solutions"
    - Our customers = Decision makers, direct end users
    - Our customers' customers = Ultimate end users

- What do customers prioritize jobs for improvements?
  - Satisfaction vs importance

- What roles are involved in a use or adoption decision?
  - End users: interact directly with product
  - Decision makers: make financial / use decisions
  - Influencers inside the company
  - Beneficiaries: customers of the business
  - Influencers outside the company
    - Key opinion leaders
    - Experts
    - Industry influencers

- Ecosystems
  - Industry ecosystems
  - Value chain ecosystems

## Decision Criteria = What “Better” Means to the Customer

- Customers assess value based on how well you help them do the job
- **Features** don’t matter unless they improve:
  - Cost
  - Speed
  - Accuracy
  - Ease of use

## Data is not Evidence Without Structure

- Interviews are just noise unless you:
  - Know what you want to learn
  - Design for **hypothesis testing**
  - Identify what would prove you wrong

## Good Hypotheses Are...

- Testable through real customer conversations  
- Specific and falsifiable  
- Relevant to your biggest risks  
- Designed to be disproven – fast

## Discovery Methods

- This is not a focus group
- It’s not a survey
- It’s not a pitch
- It’s a structured learning process through interviews

## Interview Tips

- Ask for 20 minutes
- Don’t sell – learn
- Let them teach you
- Follow your interview **goals**, not a script

## Why This is Hard

- No one gives clear answers
- You’ll face:
  - Confirmation bias
  - Social desirability bias
  - Unstructured conversations
- But learning to hear what matters is a superpower

# I-Corps Next Day 2 Notes

## Startup Mistakes — Top 10

- Hiring poorly
- Lack of focus
- Building something nobody wants
- Failure to execute sales & marketing
- Chasing investors, not customers
- Not making sure you have enough money
- Spending too much money
- Failing to ask for help
- Ignoring social media

## Evidence-Based Discovery
- Evidence comes from discovery
  - Primarily through **interviews**
  - **Data is not evidence** without structure

## Clinical Example: Cardiogenic Shock

### Determine Etiology:
- ECG, echocardiography
- Arterial line BP monitoring
- Right heart catheterization
- Lactate & organ function markers

### Pharmacological Management:
- **Pre/Early Shock**
  - SBP < 100mmHg
  - HR 70–100
  - Normal lactate
  - Cool extremities
- **Shock**
  - SBP < 90mmHg
  - HR > 100
  - Lactate > 2
  - Altered mental status
- **Severe Shock**
  - SBP < 90mmHg
  - HR > 120
  - Lactate > 4
  - Obtunded

### Underlying Causes:
- Acute MI — 32% (STEMI 59%, NSTEMI 41%)
- Cardiomyopathy — 30% (hypertrophic, dilated, autoimmune, etc.)
- Ischemic heart disease — 19%
- Other — 18%

## Testing Needs & Solutions

- Use decision criteria to test and validate value
- Understand what improvement is needed for adoption

# I-Corps Next Day 3 Notes

## What Makes a Good Beachhead Market?
- Customer has a compelling reason/need to buy
- Target customer is accessible to your sales force/channel
- Target customer is well-funded and able to pay
- You can deliver a full solution or find the right partners
- Consider if there are entrenched competitors
- Look for related/adjacent segments for future expansion
- Ensure the market aligns with the passion, interests, and goals of the founders

## Ecosystems: Industry & Value Chain
- Identify key industry players and interview targets
- Understand industry-level “jobs” and process steps
- Map evidence from customer discovery interviews
- Reveal different business model options based on direct customer choice
- Example players: EPCs, Catalyst Suppliers, Feedstock Suppliers, Off-takers,
  Regulators, Industry Associations

## Customer Stakeholder Map
- Shows typical company or consumer types
- Identifies key stakeholders and typical job titles (B2B)
- Assesses balance of influence — who has the most say
- Shows “customer of the customer” for component solutions

## Workflows
- Understand how the job is done today
- Identify key steps in larger processes
- Show “competing jobs”
- Visualize process owners and bottlenecks
- Useful for showing before/after states for improvements

## Channels
- How will you sell to, deliver to, and support customers?
- Identify direct vs. indirect sales channels
  - Direct: sell directly to end-use customer
  - Indirect: use channel partners, distributors, resellers
- Each intermediary in a channel expects to get paid
- Channel partners are motivated by money and ease
- Product complexity drives channel complexity

### Three High-Level Channel Functions
- Sales
- Distribution
- Support

## Customer Relationships
- Define how you will Get, Keep, and Grow customers
- Understand Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
  - Includes marketing and sales costs
  - Should align with solution and channel complexity
- High touch vs. low touch sales models have different CAC ranges

### Customer Acquisition Cost vs. Sales Complexity (Examples)
- Freemium/No Touch: $0–$10 CAC
- Self-Service/Inside Sales: $50–$2,000 CAC
- High Touch/Field Sales with SEs: $25,000–$200,000 CAC

## Revenue Streams & Pricing
- Identify how you will make money (asset sale, usage fees, subscription,
  licensing, rental, advertising, upsell)
- Align pricing models with customer expectations
  - Examples: cost plus, competitive pricing, volume pricing, value-based pricing
- Understand customer buying habits and budgeting cycles
- Calculate Lifetime Value (LTV) to ensure it is several multiples of CAC to
  cover costs and profit

## Key Discovery Questions to Ask Customers
- How do you typically purchase products? (asset, lease, subscription)
- How do purchases get authorized? At what price point does it get complicated?
- What justifications are needed for budget approval?
- What is the cost of not having a better solution?
- What does a typical budgeting cycle look like for a purchase like this?

## Payment Flows in the Ecosystem
- Map out how money moves between stakeholders
- Understand decision-making roles: champions, committees, OEMs, regulators
- Plan service contracts, one-time sales, and annual agreements

## Breakouts & Next Steps
- Map your channels: direct or indirect? Physical or digital? Who are
  intermediaries?
- Define your initial hypotheses for customer acquisition and revenue generation
- Use discovery interviews to validate your assumptions on market, channels,
  relationships, and pricing
